# German regional dialect recordings: licensed-source prerequisite

Research date: 2026-10-11. Scope: the Whisper-only investigation in [Qualify German fidelity and unclear-span behavior](https://github.com/comentator/tdesktop/issues/12). The approved full GGML large-v3 model and two independent human reviewers do not authorize acquisition of any corpus. This report proposes source-specific acquisition choices; no audio, model, corpus-reference archive, or private recording was acquired, played, streamed, or transcribed. Documentation and publisher metadata only were inspected. No tests/builds were run and no qualification result is claimed.

The subsequent user instruction to work without a human in the loop supersedes this report's proposed new live-review procedure. Existing published human annotation still needs honest provenance and reference-quality limits; no review or dialect competence is fabricated. See [the current autonomous artifact](whisper-german-fidelity.md) for the current scope and exercised evidence.

## Decision summary

There is no researched source that simultaneously provides unrestricted, immediately downloadable, contemporary natural conversation across all German dialects with guaranteed faithful human references. Two bounded acquisition proposals are available:

1. **Germany-specific read-dialect control:** approve only `de-ba.zip` from xSID-audio **0.2**, **209,309,866 bytes**, for local Whisper research and blind independent reference creation. It contains human recordings in **one rural Upper Bavarian speaker's variety**, not representative spontaneous conversation. Its written prompts are not verified actual-speech references. This is a concrete acquisition, but not the natural-dialogue qualification set.
2. **Explicit Swiss-German oral-history pilot:** approve only ArchiMob Release 2's **five audio samples**, provided the intended use qualifies as noncommercial and both reviewers can competently audit the sampled Swiss varieties. Publisher metadata reports a **1,752,736-byte distribution-package size**, not independently observed ZIP payload size. Exact sample filenames are available. This is much smaller and naturally spoken, but its five clips' regional coverage, duration, and phenomenon coverage have not been established. Swiss German must be a separately named dialect scope, **not a proxy for dialects spoken in Germany or Austria**.

For stronger Germany-based vocabulary/grammar coverage, request a **small, expressly permitted Betthupferl subset** from Bayerischer Rundfunk (BR); for natural dialect conversation, IDS or REDE are promising **permission-gated alternatives**, not acquisitions already approved or unrestricted open data. The report separates those access blockers below.

All corpus texts remain candidate annotations, never guaranteed ground truth. Two independent reviewers must first audit the actual speech **blind to Whisper outputs**, retain regional vocabulary/grammar, and preserve genuinely unresolved spans rather than guessing or replacing them with Standard German. The existing reviewer selection needs confirmation of competence for the *specific chosen dialect*, not simply general German fluency.

The paper also identifies ASR as a permitted application. Retain this stated research scope and exclusion; do not interpret the generic CC label as permission to disregard publisher conditions. Broader commercial use or legal reconciliation of the additional purpose restriction with CC's no-additional-restrictions language requires rights clarification. (2021)](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.197/), and [Winkler et al. (2024)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.1297/). Keep initial audio and newly authored references local; determine derivative/reference redistribution obligations before publication rather than assuming that independently typed transcripts are unrestricted.

**Reference mismatch:** v0.2 corrects a Bavarian sentence-ID misalignment and supplies `Text` (recorded prompt version) versus `Text_original` (original written dataset), including expanded abbreviations and repaired intentional grammatical errors. It makes **no audio changes**. These are publisher-written prompts. Even if the speaker generally reads them faithfully, they are not a fresh, blind human audit of what was actually uttered. German and Bavarian sentences were independently translated from English and need not align word-for-word. A Standard German counterpart cannot score retention of Bavarian lexical or grammatical forms.

**Explicit small scoring pilot proposal, not an observed manifest:** acquire only the Bavarian archive after approval, then predeclare a transparent sample such as the first 16 lexicographically ordered test WAV paths, without looking at model outputs. Record those exact paths and individual byte counts after lawful acquisition. Have the two reviewers independently create/audit faithful references before scoring. The archive is the exact approvable asset now; a smaller direct per-clip download was not established. Single-speaker, short, controlled commands may expose particular normalization examples but cannot establish dialect-wide fidelity, filler retention, or unclear-span handling. The one-speaker accent, translated prompts, quiet room, and virtual-assistant domain are material sampling biases.

## 2. ArchiMob: small Swiss-German natural-speech option

**Primary sources:** [UZH publisher page](https://www.spur.uzh.ch/en/research/projects-all/lab-projects/ArchiMob0.html), [versioned sample DOI](https://doi.org/10.48656/kdap-cy77), [SWISSUbase metadata for dataset 2770](https://www.swissubase.ch/api/v2/datasets/2770), [public download action metadata](https://www.swissubase.ch/api/v2/actions/dataset/2770), [creator methodology paper (2019)](https://bop.unibe.ch/linguistik-online/article/download/5947/8191).

The sample deposit is **“ArchiMob Release 2 (2019) – Audio samples,” version 1.0.0**, published in the repository on 2023-08-30, dataset reference **2276**, internal dataset **2770**, version UUID `eb96651bbc644741958413b5e750906a`. Its API explicitly marks **open** access and public download availability/actionability. The DOI is the acquisition landing URL; an exact payload download URL was **not** obtained. No account was created, license was accepted, or download action invoked.

Publisher metadata reports `dipSize: 1752736` and distribution MD5 `5b8c61e13e5d9065cff52ca24db01089`. Treat these as **deposit package metadata**, not proven raw ZIP bytes/checksum. The archive manifest lists these five exact assets:

- `wav_samples.zip/wav_samples/d1007_T70.wav`
- `wav_samples.zip/wav_samples/d1008_T72.wav`
- `wav_samples.zip/wav_samples/d1044_T71.wav`
- `wav_samples.zip/wav_samples/d1048_T71.wav`
- `wav_samples.zip/wav_samples/d1053_T70.wav`

The metadata says these are small speech segments from **five different participants**, whose birth years span 1905–1932. Individual clip bytes, duration, audio intelligibility and precise dialect provenance were not established. Do **not** equate five document IDs with five verified dialect regions. Metadata for these assets, including any regional provenance necessary to establish reviewer competence, must be resolved before scoring. The [full audio DOI](https://doi.org/10.48656/brdm-ht43) is a separate asset; the sample description says the complete audio is available **on request**, not part of this small proposal.

**License:** the sample metadata and UZH page identify [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en): noncommercial use only, attribution to Scherrer, Samardžić & Glaser with source/license, indicate modifications when sharing, ShareAlike for shared adaptations, and no additional downstream restrictions. This license is specific to the audio deposit, not inferred from the paper's different license. Confirm the intended Wayfinder evaluation is noncommercial; a project being open source alone does not establish that fact. No independent commercial permission was found. Other rights such as privacy are not automatically exhausted by CC licensing.

**What makes it useful:** the original source is oral-history interviews about life in Switzerland during WWII, not read prompts. Release 2 has 43 selected interviews in Swiss German, representing regional varieties **within Switzerland**. The creators manually transcribed recordings in Dieth-based dialect orthography, aligned utterances, and separately added normalization/POS annotations. They describe filled/empty pauses, repetitions/truncations and unclear/untranscribable spans in the annotation format. This documents a potential useful annotation design; it does **not** establish that the five sample clips actually contain any such phenomenon.

**Critical distinction:** ArchiMob's normalized layer is **not simply a Standard German translation**. It is a word-level lexical-identity annotation, often using German forms but retaining some reconstructed/common Swiss forms; it changes spelling distinctions and can merge morphological distinctions. Normalized attributes are therefore unsuitable as faithful actual-speech references. The original dialect transcript is more relevant, but orthographic conventions evolved across five transcribers and multiple transcription phases; creators report inconsistencies and later corrections. Independently re-audit the originals and preserve speech not recoverable by simple extraction of word nodes: pauses and repeated/truncated material occupy other XML elements. Do not flatten the corpus into a seemingly verbatim reference while dropping those elements.

**Pilot condition:** approve only this five-clip sample, not the full corpus or transcript archive, if Swiss German is explicitly in scope and both chosen reviewers can competently audit each sampled regional variety. They should create their own faithful references from raw audio, blind to model output, with a declared spelling policy. Exact-phenomenon coverage remains unknown until lawful listening. These older speakers, oral-history topics, dialect-contact exclusions and removal of some poor-quality recordings bias the sample away from general contemporary German interaction.

## 3. Betthupferl 1.0: strong Germany-based vocabulary/grammar candidate, copyright-gated

**Primary sources:** [creator paper (Interspeech 2025)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/blaschke25_interspeech.pdf), [publisher repository pinned at 71ab72c3dc230d906d9cdd6048a907b025523518](https://github.com/mainlp/betthupferl/tree/71ab72c3dc230d906d9cdd6048a907b025523518), [data statement/annotation guidelines 1.0, 2025-05-31](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mainlp/betthupferl/71ab72c3dc230d906d9cdd6048a907b025523518/data-statement_annotation-guidelines.pdf). The [GitHub commit metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/mainlp/betthupferl/commits?per_page=1) identified that revision, dated 2025-10-02.

This human **read**, professionally performed/edited children's-story corpus contains roughly four hours of dialect speech across Bavaria's seven administrative regions: East Franconian, North/Central Bavarian varieties and Swabian (Alemannic), plus about 32 minutes of Standard German. Each regional component is only about 32–37 minutes, with very few speakers. It is **not** spontaneous conversation and does not cover all Germany-based dialects. Original recordings date to the 2010s and early 2020s, despite the 2025 dataset release.

Its paired dialect transcriptions and Standard German translations explicitly annotate differences in word choice, determiner-before-name constructions, possessives, pronouns, verb constructions and word order. This makes it a better conceptual fit for dialect-vocabulary/grammar retention than a corpus providing only standard translations. Nevertheless, the dialect transcription was primarily produced by **one native Central Bavarian transcriber**, not a competent independent pair for every regional dialect. Two curators checked a random sample and corrected typos; this is not a guarantee of faithful references. About 30% of episodes used publisher scripts as a transcription starting point. The published study permits some normalized alternatives in its quality analysis; that acceptance policy must not silently replace this ticket's faithful-retention requirement.

**Access blocker:** the public repository explicitly contains **no audio**. BR must grant audio use **case by case**, depending on intended use; contact named by the creators: **Gabriele Wenger-Glemser**. There is no published open audio license, permission for our two reviewers, download URL or exact byte size. The repository requests paper citation and **no redistribution of transcripts**; reference archives are password-protected to discourage scraping, not to grant audio rights. No archive or predictions were acquired.

**Small permission request proposal:** request one Upper Bavarian episode identified in the data statement as *Billy / weltbester Biber / Ameise*, with publisher transcription/story identifiers `billy-weltbester-biber-ameise-mundart-oberbayern-1` and `billy-weltbester-biber-ameise`. These are **documented story/transcription identifiers, not verified audio filenames or URLs**. The paper says stories are typically 3–4 minutes; this exact episode's bytes/duration and feature coverage are unavailable. Ask BR for exact asset identifiers, file bytes, frozen version, local frozen-Whisper evaluation permission, lawful independent reference creation, authorized access for both reviewers and permission/limits for publishing results or excerpts. Until granted, it is a source recommendation **with an access blocker**, not a lawful acquisition already arranged.

## 4. IDS DGD/AGD: broad historical Germany-based dialect speech, restricted research use

**Primary sources:** [Zwirner corpus ZW--](https://agd.ids-mannheim.de/ZW--_extern.shtml), [DDR corpus DR--](https://agd.ids-mannheim.de/DR--_extern.shtml), [DGD system/access description](https://dgd.ids-mannheim.de/dgd/pragdb.dgd_extern.sys_desc), [AGD service terms, updated 2026-09-23](https://agd.ids-mannheim.de/konditionen.shtml).

- **ZW--, documented release 2.17 (2022-02-04):** predominantly 1955–1961 collection with later material through 1972; 5,796 externally accessible recordings, 1,077 h 15 m. Covers dialects in former West Germany and some neighboring regions, including Vorarlberg, Liechtenstein and Alsace, with displaced speakers from other areas. Narratives coexist with Standard German/colloquial contributions, elicited lists/Wenker sentences and some non-German languages. Select by actual language, locality and event type, not corpus title alone. Many source texts are explicitly **“hochsprachliche Übertragung”**, unsuitable dialect-retention references.
- **DR--, documented release 2.17 (2022-02-04):** 1,642 recordings, 385 h 13 m, 440 former-DDR locations, 1960–1968; narratives/conversations plus comparison texts and word lists. Speakers were prepared beforehand. Only 126 digital transcripts described, largely standard-orthographic with some literary transcription. Prompts/comparison text are not actual-speech reference guarantees.

DGD requires registration for research/teaching. It supports individual audio/transcript **excerpt** downloads and selected whole ZW datasets; the ZW release history identifies 18 complete datasets at its first official release. There is no need to acquire a thousand-hour archive. However, no exact small recording ID/URL/bytes was publicly established in this research and no registration was performed.

Current AGD terms permit **research, teaching and study only**; prohibit commercial use, third-party transfer, ML/LLM **training or fine-tuning**, online processing/uploads (expressly including browser AI/translation), and repository redistribution. Attribution for lawful quotation: **“Archiv für Gesprochenes Deutsch (AGD)” plus the cited document identifier**. Services undergo individual privacy/interest assessment and access is not guaranteed. WAV copies up to 30 minutes cost €2 per dataset plus a €5 server-delivery fee; this is a service price, not a license permitting redistribution.

Local frozen-model inference is not the expressly named training/finetuning ban, but the intended project eligibility and legal arrangements for two independent reviewers are **not established**. Obtain explicit clarification when needed, particularly if evaluation benefits a commercial project or reviewers would receive data as third parties. Proposed small request: one narrative segment from a named target dialect, with exact ID/bytes/access agreement supplied by AGD. Historical, locally rooted speaker selection and recording-era differences are material bias. Do not score standardized translations as faithful speech.

## 5. REDE and SXUCorpus: additional Germany-based coverage, permission/manifest blockers

**REDE:** [creator 2026 corpus paper](https://www.lrec-conf.org/proceedings/lrec2026/workshops/dialres/pdf/2026.dialres-1.15.pdf), especially §§2.6 and 5. It documents 148 Germany-based locations across major dialect regions, 3,787 recordings from 2008–2015, with guided interviews/friend conversations as well as dialect/standard translation and reading tasks. **All 780 speakers are male**, with middle-aged police officers forming the core group. Orthographic/phonetic and conversation-analytic annotations exist for subsets. Access is currently **on request after a cooperation agreement**; free-license LinguRep publication is described as **in preparation**, not an existing license. No exact small recording IDs/bytes or usable audio license were established. Request a short conversation sample from a specific named region, rather than the full corpus. Note the paper's total-duration discrepancy (text 1,542 h versus table 1,570:21:32); exact total duration is not settled here. Contemporary compared with IDS does not mean representative of all voices, genders, domains, or present-day dialects.

**SXUCorpus:** [creator 2016 paper](https://aclanthology.org/L16-1736.pdf). Upper Saxon local-TV recordings from eight stations, 1992–1996; about 500 minutes, 3,477 sentence segments, read presenters and spontaneous interview partners. Four trained dialect-familiar student annotators used minimal GAT, with another assistant checking; some dialectal terms were retained. This remains candidate annotation, not guaranteed actual-speech ground truth. Data are stated **available on request to the scientific community**; no public audio license, redistribution permission, pilot manifest or bytes were established. Low-background-noise selection, regional imbalance, and lack of unique speaker IDs constrain sampling/generalization. The paper's availability statement and paper license cannot substitute for recording rights.

## 6. Other Swiss dialect options: references and licensing require care

### SwissDial

[ETH publisher page](https://mtc.ethz.ch/publications/open-source/swiss-dial.html), [creator paper, arXiv:2103.11401v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.11401.pdf), and [current request form](https://form.jotform.com/223344961502048) document Aargau, Bern, Basel, Graubünden, Lucerne, St Gallen, Valais and Zurich, approximately three hours per dialect in the original corpus. One nonprofessional speaker per dialect translates written Standard German source sentences then **reads their translation**; material is cleaned/re-recorded to obtain quiet, consistent speech. These are real human recordings, not naturally occurring conversation. Avoid the paper's synthesis demonstrations: those are not the desired real-speech assets.

The current form advertises **version 1.1 with 7,726 additional GR recordings**, requires name/email/organization/intended use and agreement to **CC BY-NC 4.0 plus no attempt to determine speaker identity**. The form's raw HTML contains the actual terms; merely reading it submits nothing. [CC BY-NC 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/) requires noncommercial use and attribution/source/license/change notices when sharing; unlike BY-NC-SA it does not add ShareAlike. No dataset request was submitted. Total current version/archive bytes, exact licensed per-clip pilot manifest and delivery URL were not established. The publisher links a [sample repository](https://gitlab.inf.ethz.ch/ou-mtc-public/swiss-dial-samples), but public visibility alone is not proof of an independently documented sample license or a verified exact audio asset.

High German source sentences are not faithful dialect references. Written dialect translations are read prompts with possible subsequent speaker edits, not independently audited actual-speech human references. The clean single-speaker/per-dialect design and skipped hard-to-translate sentences bias coverage; it is a controlled Swiss dialect option, not a filler/unclear-span or general-German qualification source.

### STT4SG-350 / SDS-200

[SwissNLP publisher catalogue](https://swissnlp.org/home/activities/datasets/) and [STT4SG creator paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-short.150.pdf) describe 343 hours across seven Swiss dialect regions with 316 speakers. Recording procedure: display **Standard German sentences**, have participants translate and speak them in dialect. Supplied sentence text is the Standard German prompt/translation, not faithful Swiss actual-speech transcription. Collection dates are December 2021–March 2022 for test and May–November 2022 for train/validation. The authors explicitly acknowledge read-speech and prompt-induced Standard German bias and missing/underrepresented varieties and ages. The paper gives archive names `clips__test.tar` and `clips__train_valid.tar`, not exact downloadable pilot bytes.

The **current** catalogue, rather than the older paper's license wording, requires signed **META-SHARE ResearchUsageOnly NoRedistribution**, with [v3.0 agreement](https://swissnlp.org/wp2020/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/STT4SG-350-License-MS-RO-NoReD-v3.0.docx) sent to `info@swissnlp.org`; commercial arrangements require separate discussion. The agreement restricts use to the licensee's controlled **site**, own research activities, prohibits commercial/internal commercial-process use, third-party resource and derivative disclosure (including trained models), sublicensing and reconstructable public distribution, and requires preserving attribution data. Organizational members/consultants/service providers may receive informed controlled access under its definition of licensee, with a retained access list; independent reviewer access must be legally arranged, not assumed. Some derivative-distribution wording is internally qualified by prohibitions; do not infer a general right to publish newly created references.

SDS-200 has the same current request-based research-only/no-redistribution access route. Its [publisher-listed 20-utterance ZIP](https://swissnlp.org/wp2020/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/sample_20utterances_sds200.zip) is an exact sample link, **not fetched**; separate sample permission and bytes were not established and its displayed Standard German text is not faithful Swiss dialect gold. STT4SG-350-ASR's separate automatically generated Swiss transcripts are **not human references**; its different transcript license cannot grant rights to the source audio. Do not acquire hundreds of hours or score translation targets just because the corpus title says speech-to-text.

### SwissGPC exclusion

The same [SwissNLP catalogue](https://swissnlp.org/home/activities/datasets/) expressly says its authors **do not hold rights to the podcast/video contents**, and therefore release a download/weak-label pipeline rather than licensed audio. This is **not** a lawful corpus-acquisition shortcut. A pipeline/code license does not license broadcasters' recordings; weak labels are not human faithful references. No pipeline was run and no broadcast/private media was discovered or fetched.

## 7. Reference competence, contamination and what remains blocked

**Blind human reference prerequisites (proposed procedure, not performed):**

- Confirm both already-selected reviewers understand the exact sampled regional dialects, including dialect-specific lexical items and grammar; general Standard German competence is insufficient. For ArchiMob, provenance of each of the five samples remains unresolved; for xSID, rural Upper Bavarian competence is needed; one Bavarian specialist cannot automatically audit Franconian/Swabian/Swiss material.
- Independently hear the authorized raw audio without Whisper hypotheses or published model predictions, retain vocabulary/grammar, filled pauses, repetitions and false starts, and mark genuinely unclear spans with boundaries and uncertainty rather than a fabricated lexical answer.
- Reconcile human disagreements separately from model scoring. Fix a consistent dialect spelling policy, permitting equivalent spellings without silently standardizing words, morphology or syntax. Record when a span cannot be resolved by either competent human; that is not evidence that a model's guess was right.
- Source transcript/prompt/normalization layers can be compared only after blind reference creation, with their provenance disclosed. No reference set is guaranteed correct merely because a publisher calls it gold.

**Possible training contamination:** [OpenAI's large-v3 model card](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3) describes one million hours of weakly labeled and four million hours of pseudo-labeled audio, but does not supply an asset-level training manifest allowing exclusion of these sources. Older broadcast and archive recordings, ArchiMob's earlier public material, SwissDial 2021 and STT4SG's 2021–2022 recordings may overlap with model exposure; overlap is **unknown**, not proven. Betthupferl's 2025 release does not make its much older broadcast source audio new. xSID's 2024 recordings provide a later source chronology, but written prompts/templates have older origins and no asset-level training audit was done. Public evaluation use of a corpus in other papers is not proof of inclusion in this exact model's training. Do not certify an uncontaminated set from dataset release dates or reported model accuracy.

**Acquisition decisions still required:** explicit choice of Germany-based rural Upper Bavarian read control versus separate noncommercial Swiss oral-history sample; approval of the exact source/version/transfer scope; confirmation of applicable research/noncommercial eligibility and reviewer access; and dialect-competence matching. Permission-gated BR/IDS/REDE/SXU recordings additionally require publisher rights/manifest/bytes. No claim that these missing permissions were obtained is made.

**Coverage still unknown:** no audio was heard, so this research cannot identify which proposed clips actually contain dialect-specific words/grammar, fillers, hesitations, overlap, low intelligibility or unclear spans. Selecting small licensed controls is useful for exercising a local evaluation path, but cannot establish broad German fidelity, unclear-span behavior, release thresholds or ticket completion. Natural conversation and any controls must be reported by their actual bounded dialect/domain, without treating Swiss German as universal German dialect coverage.
